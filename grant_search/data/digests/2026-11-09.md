# Grant digest — 2026-11-09

Short run. One finding, and it is a better opening than any open application in
the pipeline.

## Essex County is holding $3 million it has not decided how to spend

From Essex County's own FY2026 opioid abatement report: it **spent nothing in
the current reporting period** and holds roughly **$3.0 million in unspent,
uncommitted abatement funds**. It has completed an Opioid Use Disorder Needs
Assessment and built a framework grounded in the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School
principles and the Public Health Model — but states plainly that it **has not
finalized specific spending allocations**.

**This is better than an open RFA, not worse.** There is no competition to enter
yet. An organization that engages while allocations are being decided helps
shape what gets funded; one that waits for a published RFA competes inside
parameters someone else already set. This is the earliest and most favourable
point at which a funder can be approached, and it exists precisely because the
plan is unfinished.

Two things make it a genuine fit rather than a hopeful one:

**The stated framework matches the service model.** Both the Public Health Model
and the Johns Hopkins principles favour evidence-based treatment, prevention and
recovery support over enforcement — which is this organization's entire model,
delivered with named modalities and existing court and IDRC referral pathways.

**There is a tailwind.** The State Comptroller is scrutinising misuse of opioid
settlement money by Essex-area municipalities — roughly $370,000 paid to
entertainment companies in Irvington, and a Nutley youth police academy with no
drug-specific content. Under that scrutiny, counties need credible, plainly
on-mission grantees with documented clinical approaches. That favours this
organization.

**The action:** contact Essex County Department of Health and Human Services
about the abatement strategic plan and how community providers can be included.
And ask for the needs assessment — it names the gaps the county has already
decided it has, which is a statement of need written by the funder itself.

## A monitoring source worth keeping

`nj.gov/opioidfunds/` publishes **per-county abatement reports**. They disclose
each county's unspent balance, its framework, and whether allocations are
final — which amounts to advance notice of where money is sitting undirected.
Worth checking Essex, Bergen, Union and Somerset each cycle. This is the single
best recurring source found for this organization in three months of looking.

## Where the strategy has landed

Three months of this routine has produced a clear answer about where this
organization's money is most likely to come from, and it is not where the
project started. Not federal — the two best-fitting federal programs needed
national-scale capacity or a county as applicant. Not large state awards — NJDOL
pays only by reimbursement, which a $300k organization cannot float.

It is **county opioid settlement funds and a quarterly local health foundation**:
small pools, local relationships, award sizes that match the budget, and cycles
that recur rather than vanish. Four of those are now in the pipeline, plus
Essex's undirected $3 million and HFNJ's rolling door.

None of them has a deadline in the next 90 days. All of them start with a phone
call.
